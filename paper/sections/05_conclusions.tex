\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

This study examines the energetic response of a lid-driven cavity to superelliptic bottom-wall shaping. For $n=2\to4$ at $\alpha=0$, fluid area increases by $7.93\%$, but integrated dissipation rises by only $0.0218\%$, $0.3249\%$, and $0.5909\%$ at $Re=100$, $400$, and $1000$. An area-only reference overpredicts these contrasts by factors of $364$, $24$, and $13$. Dissipation per unit fluid area decreases by about $7\%$, showing that area change alone cannot explain the response.

An exact spatial partition separates dissipation in the newly opened shoulder region from redistribution over the region common to both geometries. This $\mathrm{d}A/\mathrm{d}B$ decomposition, which constitutes the central original contribution of the present work, provides direct physical insight into the mechanism underlying the sub-extensive response: at $Re=400$, the added region occupies $7.4\%$ of the fluid area but contributes only $0.2\%$ of the reference dissipation, while the common-region redistribution is itself a near-cancellation of opposing lid-band and core-band contributions. The common-region contribution is negative at $Re=100$ and positive at $Re=400$ and $1000$, reflecting a Reynolds-dependent balance between near-wall and core changes. The net contrast remains positive throughout. The grid robustness of this mechanism is assessed in Table~SM.XV and Fig.~SM.5: all four bands are recomputed with the same exact-partition code at every available resolution ($N=128$, $256$, $384$, $512$ where available, and $768$ at $Re=100$); the cancellation index $C=\sum|\text{bands}|/|\mathrm{d}A|$ grows from $4.0$ at $Re=100$ to $14.3$ at $Re=1000$, but the net contrast $\Delta D=\mathrm{d}A+\mathrm{d}B$ remains stable to within $2.5\%$ across all grids because the grid-induced changes in $\mathrm{d}A$ and $\mathrm{d}B$ partially cancel. The sign and order of magnitude of $\mathrm{d}A$ and $\mathrm{d}B$ are therefore robust on every grid; their individual magnitudes carry a grid sensitivity that rises with $Re$ (the $\mathrm{d}A$ span is $1.9\%$, $8.2\%$ and $27\%$ of its value at $Re=100$, $400$ and $1000$). Three levels must be kept distinct here. The cell-by-cell identity $\Delta D=\mathrm{d}A+\mathrm{d}B$ is algebraically exact for the discrete fields at every $N$ and closes to machine precision. The numerical accuracy of each term is weaker, because $\mathrm{d}A$ is the residual of larger opposing lid-band and core-band contributions and its relative uncertainty is amplified by the cancellation index $C$. And the convergence statement applies to the discrete partition as a whole: the individual limits of $\mathrm{d}A$ and $\mathrm{d}B$ in the continuous domain are not separately established, whereas their sum is stable to a few percent. The physical conclusion therefore rests on the robust sum and on the signs of the two terms, not on the precise split between them. At $Re=100$, the energy-balance defect varies by $5.0$ points between $N=128$ and $N=768$ while $I_D(2,4)$ remains within $7.2\times10^{-4}$~pp (Table~SM.XIX), confirming that the defect is a common-mode error that cancels in the paired contrast.

The response is concentrated in the $n=2\to4$ transition at all Reynolds numbers studied, which carries about $96.2\%$ of the summed absolute contrasts at $Re=400$. The full exponent sequence is now available at all three Reynolds numbers: at $\alpha=0$ the $n=4\to6$ contrast is $+0.00296\%$, $+0.0129\%$, and $+0.0103\%$, and the $n=6\to8$ contrast is $+0.00042\%$, $+6\times10^{-5}\%$, and $-0.0037\%$ at $Re=100$, $400$, and $1000$. Resolution is judged transition by transition against each contrast's own $N=128$--$384$ span. At $Re=1000$ both trailing steps are resolved, the $(6,8)$ step by a factor of $34$ and with the same negative sign on all three grids, so it is reported as the first resolved negative trailing step. At $Re=400$ that step lies inside its own span, so its sign is undetermined; at $Re=100$ the $(4,6)$ step cannot be resolution-tested, because its two coarser members mix Mach regimes, and it too is quoted as an upper bound. At $\alpha=0.1$ the two trailing transitions are negative and individually resolved at both $Re=400$ and $Re=1000$, so wall undulation fixes their sign already at moderate Reynolds number. Increasing undulation amplitude also weakens the primary contrast. Across the available resolutions ($N=128$--$768$ at $Re=100$, $N=128$--$512$ at $Re=400$ and $1000$), the primary $\alpha=0$ contrast varies by at most $3.3\%$ of its own value, although absolute integrals retain about $1\%$ grid sensitivity. Independent Taylor--Hood finite-element calculations reproduce the primary contrast to $0.002$ percentage points at $Re=400$ and to $0.008$ percentage points at $Re=1000$, on the finest meshes of each solver. This agreement is a cross-solver consistency check, not a proof of convergence for either solver, because the variation of the finite-element contrast across its own meshes is larger than some FEM--LBM differences. Scope is limited to the tested superellipse family and single undulation count; the unmatched Mach numbers of the coarse $n\ge6$ runs at $Re=100$, together with residual grid sensitivity, preclude universal scaling or grid-independent claims. Numerical uncertainties on $I_D(2,4)$ are $7.2\times10^{-4}$, $2.4\times10^{-3}$, and $1.4\times10^{-2}$ percentage points at $Re=100$, $400$, and $1000$; the first value is the span over $N=128$--$768$ (the span over $N=128$--$384$ being $3.4\times10^{-4}$~pp) and the last the span over $N=128$--$512$ (the span over $N=128$--$384$ being $1.3\times10^{-2}$~pp). The trailing contrasts have their own, generally smaller spans, collected in Table~SM.X, and are $6.9\times10^{-4}$ and $1.1\times10^{-4}$ percentage points at $Re=1000$, well below the corresponding magnitudes.

Finally, the crossover of the local exponent ($\gamma_{\mathrm{loc}}=1$) near $Re\approx346$, observed in the exploratory $N=128$ Reynolds sweep where $Ma$ reaches $0.18$, is a descriptive feature of that coarse-grid trajectory and not a converged result; no conclusion depends on its precise location.

A Richardson extrapolation of $D^*$ and $P^*$ (Section~VII of the Supplementary Material) gives effective orders between $0.70$ and $1.07$ that are not stable under removal of a single grid, so no converged limit or grid-convergence index is claimed. The extrapolated contrast is also not robust: at $Re=100$ the four-level fit gives $+0.0233\%$, against $+0.0191\%$ with the three production grids and $+0.0257\%$ when the coarsest grid is dropped, a spread of a factor $1.35$. No extrapolated contrast is therefore quoted as a result. The dominant residual uncertainty is the lid-layer velocity gradient at coarse resolution: a second-order reconstruction changes $D^*$ by $-17\%$ at $N=128$, but by only $-0.24\%$ on the converged $N=768$ field, and an independent non-equilibrium MRT estimate agrees with the production finite-difference value to $0.16\%$ globally and $0.2\%$ on the lid row. The production (first-order) value is retained as the reported number.

Several targeted extensions would quantify what the present design deliberately leaves as a conservative span, and none is needed for the contrast hierarchy reported here. On the LBM side, extending the $N=768$ level to $Re=400$ and $Re=1000$ would complement the $Re=100$ fourth level already computed and show whether the $N=256\to384$ changes of $D^*$ and $P^*$ continue to decrease at the higher Reynolds numbers. On the FEM side, a $Re=100$ Taylor--Hood calculation would test the cross-solver agreement at the low-Reynolds end, where the contrast is smallest. A converged Richardson fit---one whose effective order is stable under removal of the coarsest grid---would replace the present span by an extrapolated value with an explicit grid-convergence index; the present data do not provide such an order, and no extrapolated contrast is quoted as a result. Finally, the crossover $Re\approx346$ remains a descriptive feature of the coarse $N=128$ sweep; a finer-grid Reynolds sweep would establish whether it survives refinement. These are perspectives only: no new simulation, extrapolated integral, or additional physical conclusion is used in the present work.
