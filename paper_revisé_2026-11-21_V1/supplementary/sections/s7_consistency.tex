\section{Energetic consistency and resolution sensitivity}
\label{suppsec:consistency}

\subsection{Global energy-balance audit}

For each converged simulation, the signed relative imbalance between power input and viscous dissipation is
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon_E = \frac{P^*-D^*}{D^*}\times 100\%.
\end{equation}
The defect is negative in every case ($P^*<D^*$), between $-1.98\%$ and $-2.17\%$ in magnitude at $N=384$, and it decreases with grid refinement as a first-order (in $1/N$) discretisation error rather than saturating: at $Re=400$, $n=2$, $\alpha=0$ it is $-6.20\%$ ($N=128$), $-3.20\%$ ($N=256$), and $-2.04\%$ ($N=384$), whose ratios $1.94$ and $1.57$ are close to the $2$ and $1.5$ expected for an error proportional to the mesh spacing. The $N=768$ extension continues the decay, reaching $-0.86\%$ at $Re=100$, $-0.89\%$ at $Re=400$ and $-0.85\%$ at $Re=1000$ for $n=2$. It is not an iterative-convergence error, since the velocity change at the final iteration is at or below $9.9\times10^{-9}$ in every case. Because the residual decays as $O(1/N)$, it is a resolution-limited defect of the absolute integrals rather than a fixed estimator offset. It measures the mismatch between the injected power and the computed dissipation; it does not by itself bound the error on $D^*$, and no grid-independent claim is made for the absolute integrals. The matched contrasts (differences of same-grid states) are instead stable to better than $1\%$ between $N=256$ and $N=384$ and to within a few percent over all available resolutions (Section~\ref{suppsec:consistency}). Table~\ref{supptab:balance} reports $\varepsilon_E$ for each case at $N=384$.

\subsection{Discrepancy between power- and dissipation-based contrasts}

The agreement between the power-based and dissipation-based estimates of the same contrast is quantified by $\delta_{IP/ID} = 100|I_P/I_D-1|$. At $N=384$ for the transition $(2,4)$, $\alpha=0$, the values are $3.14\%$ ($Re=100$), $1.04\%$ ($Re=400$), and $1.91\%$ ($Re=1000$). For $(4,6)$ at $Re=400$ the discrepancy rises to $10.3\%$, and for $(6,8)$ it is not meaningful because both contrasts are of order $10^{-4}\%$ and their ratio is dominated by cancellation. These values indicate that the two independent routes to the dominant contrast are consistent at this resolution. For the trailing transitions at $Re=1000$, where both contrasts are resolved, the discrepancy is $40.4\%$ for $(4,6)$ ($I_D=+0.0103\%$, $I_P=+0.0062\%$) and $15.6\%$ for $(6,8)$ ($I_D=-0.0037\%$, $I_P=-0.0043\%$); the two estimators still agree in sign for $(6,8)$ but the ratio is not a precision indicator at these magnitudes. At $\alpha=0.1$ and $Re=1000$ the same two transitions give $4.7\%$ and $8.1\%$. This estimator consistency is distinct from the grid-sensitivity analysis: agreement between $I_P$ and $I_D$ at $N=384$ does not imply that the contrast itself is grid-converged.

\subsection{Resolution sensitivity}

At $\alpha=0$ the primary contrast takes the values $I_D(2,4)=0.02161\%$, $0.02195\%$, $0.02178\%$ at $Re=100$; $0.32251\%$, $0.32462\%$, $0.32488\%$, $0.32487\%$, $0.32442\%$ at $Re=400$; and $0.57758\%$, $0.58769\%$, $0.59087\%$, $0.59176\%$, $0.59201\%$ at $Re=1000$ (values at $N=128$, $256$, $384$, $512$, $768$; $N=512$ available only at $Re=400$ and $1000$), the target relaxation time being held fixed per Reynolds number. The fifth level, $N=768$, is converged at all three Reynolds numbers (residuals below $10^{-8}$) and gives $+0.02232\%$, $+0.32442\%$ and $+0.59201\%$. Between $N=256$ and $N=384$ the relative changes are $-0.73\%$ ($Re=100$), $+0.08\%$ ($Re=400$), and $+0.54\%$ ($Re=1000$). The sequence rises monotonically up to $N=384$ at both $Re=400$ and $Re=1000$; thereafter the $N=512$ value is essentially unchanged at $Re=400$ (a decrease of $9\times10^{-6}$ percentage points) and continues to rise at $Re=1000$. At $Re=100$ the sequence is not monotone at the fourth significant figure: over $N=128$--$384$ the span is $3.4\times10^{-4}$ percentage points ($1.6\%$ of the contrast), and the $N=768$ value lies $2.5\%$ above the $N=384$ one ($5.4\times10^{-4}$~pp), so that the four-level span is $7.2\times10^{-4}$~pp ($3.2\%$ of the finest-grid value). The contrast is therefore stable to within a few percent of its value over the available resolutions at all three Reynolds numbers ($N=128$--$768$ at all three Reynolds numbers), with spans of $7.2\times10^{-4}$~pp ($Re=100$), $2.4\times10^{-3}$~pp ($Re=400$), and $1.4\times10^{-2}$~pp ($Re=1000$).

The absolute dissipation is less well converged. For $n=2$, $\alpha=0$, $D^*$ changes by $+0.79\%$ ($Re=100$), $+0.78\%$ ($Re=400$), and $+0.93\%$ ($Re=1000$) between $N=256$ and $N=384$, and by a further $+0.86\%$, $+0.84\%$ and $+0.92\%$ between $N=384$ and $N=768$ at $Re=100$, $400$ and $1000$. The absolute integrals therefore retain a residual grid dependence of order $1\%$ at the finest resolution available. These changes are larger than those of the contrast, which reflects the partial cancellation of common discretization error in the paired difference. No claim of grid-independent absolute energetics is made. The conservative numerical uncertainties on $I_D(2,4)$ quoted in the main text ($7.2\times10^{-4}$, $2.4\times10^{-3}$, and $1.4\times10^{-2}$ percentage points at $Re = 100$, $400$, and $1000$) are the spans of $I_D(2,4)$ over the available resolutions ($N = 128$--$768$ at all three Reynolds numbers) reported in Table~\ref{supptab:contrasts}; they are upper bounds, as the sequence rises monotonically up to $N=384$ at all three Reynolds numbers, with the $N=512$ value essentially unchanged at $Re=400$ and still rising at $Re=1000$. At $Re=100$ the $N=768$ level supersedes $N=384$ as the finest grid and widens the span from $3.4\times10^{-4}$ to $7.2\times10^{-4}$~pp; the $N=128$--$384$ span is quoted separately wherever the distinction matters.

At $Re=1000$ the full exponent sequence is resolved at $N=384$: $(2,4)=+0.5909\%$, $(4,6)=+0.0103\%$, $(6,8)=-0.0037\%$. The $N=512$ data at $Re=1000$ confirm the monotonicity of the $(2,4)$ contrast ($+0.592\%$ at $N=512$) and strengthen the negative sign of the $(6,8)$ transition ($-0.0036\%$ at $N=512$); at $N=768$ the primary contrast reaches $+0.5920\%$ while the trailing transitions remain unavailable at that resolution. Unlike the $Re=400$ trailing transitions, these two lie well above their own $N=128$--$512$ spans: $(4,6)$ takes $+0.01074\%$, $+0.01058\%$, $+0.01033\%$, $+0.01005\%$ and $(6,8)$ takes $-0.00371\%$, $-0.00360\%$, $-0.00371\%$, $-0.00364\%$ at $N=128$, $256$, $384$, $512$, i.e.\ spans of $6.9\times10^{-4}$ and $1.1\times10^{-4}$ percentage points, each more than twenty times smaller than the corresponding magnitude, and the sign of $(6,8)$ is the same on all four grids. The negative trailing contrast is therefore a resolved Reynolds-dependent effect and not a resolution artefact; it is also insensitive to the amplitude tested, $\alpha=0.1$ giving $-0.0052\%$ with a span of $5.5\times10^{-4}$ percentage points. At $Re=400$ the same $(6,8)$ transition lies inside its own span ($+0.00006\%$ against $1.1\times10^{-4}$) and changes sign between $N=128$ and $N=256$, which is why it is quoted there only as an upper bound.

\subsection{Spatial decomposition of the geometric contrast}

The global contrast between $n=4$ and $n=2$ is localized exactly by partitioning the $n=4$ fluid region into the part $A$ common to both geometries and the part $B$ added by flattening. Writing $\ell$ for the local dissipation density times the cell area, the identity
\begin{equation}
\Delta D=D^*(4,0)-D^*(2,0)=\underbrace{\sum_{A}(\ell_4-\ell_2)}_{\mathrm{d}A}+\underbrace{\sum_{B}\ell_4}_{\mathrm{d}B}
\end{equation}
holds cell by cell, because $A$ and $B$ are disjoint and their union is the whole $n=4$ region. The two terms and their sum are listed in Table~\ref{supptab:layers}; the sum $\mathrm{d}A+\mathrm{d}B$ reproduces the headline contrast $I_D(2,4)$ to machine precision, and the four bands of the common region (lid, bottom, side/corner, interior core) are disjoint and sum to $\mathrm{d}A$ exactly. The interior core is negative at all three $Re$; the lid and bottom bands are positive for $Re\ge400$ and weakly negative at $Re=100$, where the largest common-region contribution is the side/corner band. The added-region term $\mathrm{d}B$ is positive at all $Re$ and grows monotonically. The redistribution term $\mathrm{d}A$ is negative only at $Re=100$ and crosses zero near $Re\approx145$ (Table~\ref{supptab:dadb}), so the net contrast $I_D(2,4)$ is positive at all Reynolds numbers while only $\mathrm{d}A$ changes sign.

\paragraph{Choice of band thresholds.}
The four-band decomposition of the common region $A$ uses thresholds chosen to isolate the physically distinct zones where dissipation concentrates or where the geometric change has its strongest effect. The \emph{lid band} (top $10\%$ of the cavity, $y>0.9$) captures the shear layer driven by the moving lid, which contains $74$--$84\%$ of the total dissipation depending on $Re$ (Section~IV.C of the main text). The \emph{bottom band} (below $y_b+0.1$, where $y_b(x)$ is the bottom-wall height) isolates the near-wall region directly affected by the superelliptic shaping; at $0.1$ in wall distance it extends just far enough above the bottom to capture the wall-normal dissipation gradient without overlapping the core. The \emph{side/corner zone} (within $0.1$ of any vertical wall) captures the lateral boundary layers and the rounded-corner fillets where the Bouzidi treatment resolves the curved geometry. The \emph{interior core} is the remainder: the bulk recirculation zone away from all walls. These thresholds ($10\%$, $0.1$) are not tuned to fit a particular result; they are round numbers of order $10\%$ that separate the cavity into its natural flow regions. A sensitivity test at $Re=400$ with alternative thresholds ($8\%$ and $12\%$ for the lid band, $0.08$ and $0.12$ for the bottom/side zones) changes the individual band contributions by at most $15\%$ but leaves the sum $\mathrm{d}A$ unchanged to machine precision (by construction, since the bands are disjoint and exhaustive) and the total contrast $I_D(2,4)$ identical. The physical interpretation---lid-dominated dissipation with a weakly dissipative added region---is therefore robust to the exact threshold values, which serve only to organize the accounting, not to determine it.

\paragraph{Two-level interpretation of the mechanism.}
The $\mathrm{d}A/\mathrm{d}B$ decomposition supports two levels of conclusion, with different robustness to grid resolution:

\emph{Robust (qualitative) level:} (i)~The sign of $\mathrm{d}B$ is positive at all $Re$ and all $N$, confirming that the newly opened shoulder region always adds dissipation. (ii)~The sign of $\mathrm{d}A$ is negative at $Re=100$ and positive at $Re\geq 400$, confirming that the common-region redistribution changes sign near $Re\approx 145$. (iii)~At $Re=100$, where $\mathrm{d}A<0$ and $\mathrm{d}B>0$, the net contrast is much smaller than either term; at $Re\ge400$ both terms are positive and comparable in magnitude to $\Delta D$, but $\mathrm{d}A$ is itself a near-cancellation of much larger lid-band and core-band contributions. The sub-extensive response therefore reflects a weakly dissipative added region together with an internal cancellation \emph{within} $\mathrm{d}A$, not a cancellation \emph{between} $\mathrm{d}A$ and $\mathrm{d}B$. (iv)~The dissipation is concentrated in the lid band at $Re\geq 400$, where it accounts for $74$--$84\%$ of the total.

\emph{Quantitative level:} The individual magnitudes of the four bands and of $\mathrm{d}A$ carry a grid sensitivity that grows with $Re$: $\mathrm{d}A$ spans $0.09$ units ($1.9\%$ of itself) at $Re=100$, $1.05$ units ($8.2\%$) at $Re=400$, and $2.81$ units ($27\%$) at $Re=1000$ across the available resolutions ($N=128$--$384$ at $Re=100$, $N=128$--$512$ at $Re=400$ and $1000$). This reflects the internal cancellation, quantified by the rising index $C$. The net contrast $\Delta D$ remains bounded to $2.4\%$ over the same resolution ranges. The quantitative mechanism---the exact balance between lid-band gain and core-band loss---is resolved at the finer grids ($N\ge256$, where the band values have settled to within a few percent); the $N=128$ bands are order-of-magnitude estimates.

\paragraph{Alternative two-zone decomposition.}
We also define an alternative \emph{near-wall} zone (lid $+$ bottom $+$ side bands) and \emph{bulk} zone (core band), to check how the four-band split affects the conclusion. At $Re=400$, $N=384$, this gives $\mathrm{d}A_{\text{near-wall}} = +41.07$ and $\mathrm{d}A_{\text{bulk}} =-28.29$, with $C_{\text{alt}} = (41.07 + 28.29)/12.78 = 5.4$; here $C_{\text{alt}}$ coincides with the four-band $C$ because all three near-wall bands are positive, so regrouping them leaves the summed absolute contributions unchanged. The net $\mathrm{d}A = +12.78$ is unchanged. At $Re=1000$, $\mathrm{d}A_{\text{near-wall}} = +76.41$ and $\mathrm{d}A_{\text{bulk}} = -65.97$, giving $\mathrm{d}A = +10.44$ and $C_{\text{alt}} = 13.6$, slightly below the four-band value $14.1$ because the side band is negative at this Reynolds number ($-2.17$), so regrouping it with the lid and bottom bands lowers the summed absolute contributions. The qualitative conclusion---that $\mathrm{d}A$ is a near-cancellation between opposing near-wall and bulk contributions---holds for both splits; the four-band split additionally localizes the cancellation within the near-wall region (between lid, bottom, and side) and the bulk (core).

\subsection{Robustness tests}

\emph{Wall-gradient treatment.} Replacing the geometric wall-distance correction of the bottom-wall one-sided gradient by a purely node-based one-sided difference changes $I_D(2,4)$ from $0.0218\%$ to $0.0207\%$ ($Re=100$), $0.3249\%$ to $0.3212\%$ ($Re=400$), and $0.5909\%$ to $0.5777\%$ ($Re=1000$); the contrast changes by $5.0\%$ of its own value at $Re=100$ and by only $1.1$--$2.2\%$ at $Re=400$--$1000$.

\emph{Effective-Reynolds perturbation.} A $0.4\%$ differential shift of the effective Reynolds number between the two geometries propagates to a change of $0.0005\%$ in $I_D(2,4)$ at $Re=100$ ($2\%$ of its value) and $0.0011\%$ at $Re=400$ ($0.4\%$).

\emph{Grid span.} Because the residual change between the two finest grids is dominated by the partial cancellation of common discretization error rather than by a clean power-law decay, no Richardson extrapolation or GCI is quoted as a converged limit (an indicative fit is reported in Sec.~\ref{suppsec:richardson}); the span of $I_D(2,4)$ over the three production grids is $3.4\times10^{-4}$ percentage points at $Re=100$, $2.4\times10^{-3}$ at $Re=400$ and $1.3\times10^{-2}$ at $Re=1000$, i.e.\ at most $2.2\%$ of the finest-grid contrast. With $N=768$ included, the spans become $2.4\times10^{-3}$~pp at $Re=400$ and $1.4\times10^{-2}$~pp ($2.5\%$ of the finest-grid value) at $Re=1000$, both over $N=128$--$768$; at $Re=100$ the span widens from $3.4\times10^{-4}$ to $7.2\times10^{-4}$~pp ($3.2\%$). The absolute integrals still change by $0.84\%$ ($Re=400$) and $0.92\%$ ($Re=1000$) between $N=384$ and $N=768$, so the $0.2\%$ two-finest-grid criterion is not met and the finest level is reported as a resolution extension. The resolution ranges are quoted explicitly wherever the uncertainty is used.

\emph{Discrete versus analytic area.} The discrete fluid area differs from the analytic value of \texttt{area\_fluid} by at most $0.017\%$ over all cases, so the choice of discrete or analytic reference area does not affect any quoted contrast.


\subsection{Richardson extrapolation of the absolute integrals}
\label{suppsec:richardson}

A three-parameter fit $Q(N)=Q_\infty+C\,N^{-p}$ was applied to the absolute integrals $D^*$ and $P^*$ for $n=2$ and $n=4$, $\alpha=0$, using the available sequences ($N=128,256,384,768$ at $Re=100$; $N=128,256,384,512$ at $Re=400$ and $1000$). The $N=768$ point is not used in these fits, so that the reported orders remain comparable across Reynolds numbers. The fitted effective order is not stable. At $Re=100$, where four levels are now available, $D^*$ gives $p=0.748$ for both $n=2$ and $n=4$, with $Q_\infty$ $1.28\%$ above $D^*(768)$; removing the coarsest grid gives $p=0.82$ and $Q_\infty$ $1.12\%$ above the finest value, and removing the finest grid gives $p=0.70$ and $Q_\infty$ $2.40\%$ above it. The order therefore ranges from $0.70$ to $0.82$ and the extrapolated limit from $1.1\%$ to $2.4\%$ above the finest $D^*$, so the four-level sequence is still not in an asymptotic regime. Across Reynolds numbers the order for $D^*$ is $0.84$ at $Re=400$ and $1.07$ at $Re=1000$; for $P^*$ it is $0.69$, $0.78$ and $0.85$ at $Re=100$, $400$ and $1000$. Repeating the fit after removing the coarsest grid changes $Q_\infty$ by up to $1.2\%$ for $D^*$ and $0.8\%$ for $P^*$, and the $N=64$ point available for $n=4$, $Re=400$ drives the fit to its lower bound $p=0.5$, which is why that point is excluded from the reported values. The extrapolated limits are therefore indicative only. The contrast $I_D(2,4)$ extrapolates to $+0.0233\%$ at $Re=100$ with the four levels, to $+0.0191\%$ when $N=768$ is removed and to $+0.0257\%$ when $N=128$ is removed, a spread of a factor $1.35$; at $Re=400$ and $1000$ the three-level sequences give $0.32493\%$ and $0.59462\%$, within $0.02\%$ and $0.48\%$ of the finest-grid values. A direct fit of $I_D(N)$ rather than of the two integrals is degenerate---the exponent runs to its imposed bounds and the extrapolated contrast moves by more than $25\%$---so the contrast is not extrapolated at all. Because the effective order is neither constant across Reynolds numbers nor stable under removal of one grid, no grid-convergence index is quoted; the observed spans remain the reported conservative uncertainties. The extrapolated values above are provided only to show the size of the residual discretisation error, not as converged limits.

\emph{Wall-gradient sensitivity.} The production diagnostics use a first-order half-cell correction for the lid-row normal velocity gradient, $(U_{\rm lid}-u_{N-1})/(\Delta/2)$. Replacing it by a second-order one-sided reconstruction that uses the prescribed lid value and the two nearest interior nodes (distances $\Delta/2$ and $3\Delta/2$ from the wall, formula $(4U_{\rm lid}-3u_{N-1}-u_{N-2})/(3\Delta)$) changes $D^*$ by $-17\%$ on a \emph{coarse} $Re=400$, $N=128$ field, where a single row carries most of the lid-layer dissipation. On the converged $Re=100$, $n=2$, $N=768$ field the same substitution changes $D^*$ by only $-0.24\%$ (and $P^*$ by $-0.78\%$ when the same stencil is applied to both diagnostics), because the lid layer then spans many rows and the top-row gradient is no longer dominant. The two stencils are verified on analytic profiles with the exact node placement of the solver (wall at $y=1$, first node at $y=1-\Delta/2$): both are exact for the linear Couette profile, and the second-order stencil is exact for quadratic and cubic profiles where the first-order one carries errors of $0.03\%$ and $0.07\%$ at $N=768$. The $-17\%$ figure is therefore a coarse-grid effect, not a systematic bias of the production value, and it is quoted here only to bound the sensitivity at low resolution. The volume-fraction integration of the cut cells at the analytic bottom wall changes $D^*$ by less than $0.04\%$. The production (first-order) value is retained as the reported number.

\emph{Non-equilibrium dissipation cross-check.} The MRT non-equilibrium estimator $D^*_{\mathrm{fneq}}$ is computed from the non-equilibrium moments of the exact collision operator, $S_{\mathrm{lat}}=-\Pi^{\mathrm{neq}}/(2\rho c_s^2\tau)$, where the shear moments 7 and 8 of the MRT matrix are relaxed at $1/\tau$ (Sec.~II.A). On the near-converged $Re=100$, $n=4$, $N=768$ field (residual $2.5\times10^{-8}$) it gives $D^*_{\mathrm{fneq}}=18.102$ against $D^*_{\nabla u}=18.074$ from the production finite differences, a difference of $+0.16\%$ ($+0.11\%$ after removing the trace). The agreement is local as well as global: in the top $10\%$ of the cavity the mean shear component $S_{xy}$ agrees to $0.8\%$ with a correlation of $0.956$, and on the lid row itself to $0.2\%$; the top-decade band carries $83.99\%$ of $D^*_{\mathrm{fneq}}$ versus $83.96\%$ of $D^*_{\nabla u}$. Using $2\rho c_s^2(\tau-1/2)$ instead of $2\rho c_s^2\tau$ would inflate $D^*_{\mathrm{fneq}}$ by a factor of $19$, which confirms that the viscous relaxation rate enters with $\tau$, not $\nu$. The boundary-adjacent row, where the moving-lid momentum injection acts, is therefore consistent with the finite-difference strain to $0.2\%$; this supports the production first-order wall gradient and indicates that the $-17\%$ second-order discrepancy is confined to coarse grids. The implementation is available in \texttt{diag\_fneq.py} (in \texttt{provenance/Lid\_driven\_ondulation/}) and is activated by \texttt{LBM\_DIAG\_FNEQ=1}.

\emph{High-resolution extension.} The $N=768$ level for $n=2$ and $n=4$ ($\alpha=0$) is converged at all three Reynolds numbers. At $Re=100$ the runs give: $n=2$ gives $D^*=18.070223$ (residual $9.7\times10^{-9}$, $1.17\times10^{6}$ iterations in a single uninterrupted run) and $n=4$ gives $D^*=18.074257$ (residual $9.9\times10^{-9}$), the latter completed in a final leg of $3.8\times10^{5}$ iterations after resuming from a checkpoint (the solver restarts its iteration counter at each restart, so no cumulative total is quoted for that run). The primary contrast at this level is $I_D(2,4)=+0.022323\%$, i.e.\ $+5.4\times10^{-4}$ percentage points ($+2.5\%$ in relative terms) above the $N=384$ value, and the four-level span is $7.2\times10^{-4}$~pp ($3.2\%$). This level is a resolution \emph{extension}, not a converged mesh: the absolute integrals still change by $0.87\%$ between $N=384$ and $N=768$, so the $0.2\%$ two-finest-grid criterion is not met. The exact partition shows why the contrast is far better behaved than the integrals. At $N=768$, $\mathrm{d}B=+6.96$ and $\mathrm{d}A=-4.73$ in units of $10^{-4}D^*(2,0)$, against $+6.99$ and $-4.81$ at $N=384$ and $-4.73$ and $+6.89$ at $N=128$: each term varies by less than $2\%$ over four grids, while their sum moves by $2.5\%$, and the cancellation index stays in the narrow range $3.9$--$4.0$. The $N=384\to768$ displacement is therefore a small residual of two $O(18)$ integrals that cancel, not a Mach or Reynolds effect: the fixed-$(Re,N)$ Mach sweeps (Tables~\ref{supptab:fitma} and~\ref{supptab:ma_low_high}) change the contrast by at most $1.5\%$ over a factor-eight change of $Ma$, and refining lowers $Ma$. The four-level sequence resolves the intermediate-checkpoint excursion: that checkpoint (about $1.4\times10^{6}$ iterations into the run, residual $\sim6\times10^{-7}$) gave $I_D(2,4)\simeq+0.0259\%$, $19\%$ above the $N=384$ value, whereas the converged run gives $+2.5\%$. The intermediate value was a convergence artefact amplified by the cancellation of $\mathrm{d}A$ and $\mathrm{d}B$, which is why no number taken from an unconverged field is quoted as a result anywhere in this work. At $Re=400$ and $Re=1000$ the $N=768$ runs give $D^*=23.62949$ and $31.83541$ for $n=2$, and $23.70615$ and $32.02387$ for $n=4$, with residuals below $10^{-8}$; the primary contrasts are $+0.32442\%$ and $+0.59201\%$, against $+0.32488\%$ and $+0.59087\%$ at $N=384$. The Taylor--Hood FEM runs at $Re=1000$ for $n=2$ and $n=4$ up to $n_x=256$ are complete (Section~\ref{suppsec:crosscheck}).
